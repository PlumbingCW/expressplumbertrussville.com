What to do when a hidden leak cannot wait

Wet drywall, water stains spreading on a ceiling, a hissing sound in a wall or the sound of running water when everything is off all point to a leak that is getting worse. Our line is open 24/7 and we book the earliest visit we can.

While you wait:

  1. Shut off the main water valve if water is visibly coming through a ceiling or wall.
  2. Do not cut into drywall to look. Opening the wrong spot costs money and the real leak is often several feet away.
  3. Turn off power to a wet ceiling area if you can do so safely, and keep people out of the room.
  4. Photograph the damage and note when you first saw it. Both help your insurer and help us narrow the search.

How we find a leak without tearing out the house

Water moves along pipes, joists and slab edges before it shows up, so the stain is rarely the leak. We use acoustic listening gear that hears the hiss of pressurized water in a pipe, thermal imaging that shows temperature differences from moisture and hot lines, and moisture meters that confirm what the first two suggest.

Once we have narrowed it to a spot, we confirm with a pressure test on the suspect line. Then you get a repair price for that exact location, so the hole in your wall is as small as it can be.

When the water bill is the first sign

A bill that jumps with no change in how many people live in the house is often a leak. A quick test helps: turn off every tap and appliance, then look at your water meter. If the dial or the small leak indicator keeps moving, water is going somewhere.

A toilet that runs after you flush is the most common culprit, and a quiet flapper leak can cost a surprising amount over a month. If the meter test points to a leak and you cannot see one, call us and we will trace it, whether it is in the house or between the meter and the house.

What affects the price of leak detection

Ask for a clear price before work starts. Detection is priced by the effort to find the leak, not by guesswork.

Size of the search

A single bathroom is quick. A whole house or a long yard line takes longer.

Equipment needed

Basic listening gear is one thing, and thermal imaging or tracer testing adds to the visit.

Access

Finished ceilings and tiled floors limit where we can listen.

Repair afterward

Finding the leak and fixing it are separate prices, and you can decide on each.

Timing

Emergency calls at night or on weekends are quoted as emergency work.

Insurance may cover the repair of resulting damage from a sudden leak, but gradual leaks are often excluded, and some policies pay for the cost of finding it. Ask your agent.

Where the numbers on leaks come from

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, the average household's leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year. The EPA recommends checking your meter with all water off, which is the test we describe above.

Finding a leak early keeps both the bill and the damage small.
