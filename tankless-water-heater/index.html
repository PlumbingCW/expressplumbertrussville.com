Tankless Water Heater in Trussville, AL

Tankless units heat water as you use it. They save space and can last a long time, and they need the right gas line and venting.

Call (205) 896-0120

How a tankless heater works

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor fires the burner or the electric elements and the water heats as it passes through. There is no tank of stored hot water, so you will not run out from a long shower, but there is a limit to how many taps one unit can serve at once.

That limit, measured in gallons per minute, is what sizing is all about. A household that runs two showers and a dishwasher together needs a larger unit than one person living alone.

Tank or tankless

Neither is always better. Here is how they compare.

TankTankless
Hot water supplyLimited by tank sizeContinuous, limited by flow.
SpaceNeeds floor spaceWall mounted, compact.
Gas line and ventOften reusableOften needs upsizing and new venting.
UpkeepOccasional flushingRegular descaling in hard water.

For a conventional replacement, see water heater installation.

Why the gas line matters

Gas tankless units draw a lot of gas for a short burst. An older house with a small gas line may need a larger pipe run from the meter, and the unit needs venting rated for its exhaust.

Trussville Gas and Water supplies natural gas, so ask the utility about service capacity. We look at your gas line, the vent route and the clearances before we quote the work, because these details decide whether the job is simple or involved.

Electric tankless is a different story

Electric tankless units need a lot of electrical capacity. Many whole-house models need large dedicated circuits, and an older panel may not have room for them.

Smaller point-of-use units can serve a single sink or shower with fewer demands. We check your panel and your hot water demand before recommending either type.

Is tankless right for your house

Tankless fits best where space is tight, where hot water demand is steady, or where a stored tank has been a source of trouble. It fits less well where the gas line is small and upgrading it would cost more than the heater. Owners in a newer community such as Stockton can read our notes on gas safety and hot water demand.

If you are on the fence, we can walk the house with you and compare the tank and tankless options on your own numbers. We do not push a model that does not fit.

Hard water and descaling

Trussville Gas and Water's 2026 report lists total hardness as 205, and the table does not print a unit. The water comes from eight wells in limestone and chert aquifers, which is the kind of source that can leave scale in a heat exchanger.

Scale reduces flow and efficiency over time. Most manufacturers recommend periodic flushing with a descaling solution, and an isolation valve kit makes that easy. If the water in your area is hard, plan on regular upkeep.

What a tankless installation involves

Plans vary by house, but the work follows a pattern.

  1. We confirm capacity, gas line size and the vent route.
  2. The old tank is drained and removed, and the new unit is mounted on the wall.
  3. Gas, water, vent and condensate connections are made, with isolation valves for future flushing.
  4. We test, set the temperature and show you how to maintain it.

Gas, venting and the codes that govern them

A gas tankless unit is installed to the manufacturer's instructions and the National Fuel Gas Code, NFPA 54, which covers gas piping and venting. Plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC cover the water side.

The vent type and clearances in the manual are not optional, and an inspection confirms they were followed.
