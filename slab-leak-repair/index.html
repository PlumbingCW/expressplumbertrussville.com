What to do when a slab leak is an emergency

Three signs are classic. Water pressure drops for no reason, a patch of tile or concrete floor feels warm, or you hear running water under the house when every tap is off. Water bubbling up through the floor or flooding a room is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7 for it. We book the earliest visit we can.

Until we arrive:

  1. Close the main water valve to stop the flow. If the house has no pressure after that, the leak is almost certainly on the house side.
  2. Keep rugs and furniture off a wet floor and wipe up standing water so it does not run into walls.
  3. Stay away from outlets and appliances in a wet room, and shut off the breaker for that area only if you can do it dry.
  4. Check your water meter with everything off. A moving dial confirms a leak and helps us plan.

What a slab leak is and why it matters

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete floor. Hot water lines, because of the heat and movement, fail more often, but cold lines leak too. A small leak wastes water and raises the bill. A larger one can soften the soil beneath the slab and lead to damp floors, mold and damaged flooring.

Not every Trussville home sits on a slab, and many have basements or crawl spaces where lines are exposed and easy to reach. If yours has a slab, treat unexplained moisture or a hot spot as a reason to call, and do not wait for the stain to spread.

Ways to fix a slab leak

Breaking the floor is not the only option. The right fix depends on the line, the location and the flooring.

We explain each option and its trade-offs. Rerouting avoids cutting a finished floor, and we will say so when it is the better answer.

How we find the leak under the floor

Acoustic listening gear picks up the hiss of pressurized water through concrete. Thermal imaging helps when a hot line leaks, because the warm patch shows on the surface. Pressure testing isolates one line at a time, so we know which one to open.

That work is the same as general leak detection, applied to a slab. Locating the leak within a small area means a smaller opening and a lower repair price, and it keeps your flooring damage to a minimum.

What affects the cost of slab leak repair

Ask for a clear price before work starts. These factors drive it.

Detection time

A tight, quiet house makes listening easier than one with noisy equipment nearby.

Repair method

Spot repair, a reroute and a repipe are three different scopes.

Flooring

Tile, hardwood and carpet each add different restoration costs after the opening.

Pipe condition

If other lines are failing, a larger fix may save money in the end.

Timing

Emergency calls at night are quoted as emergency work.

Insurers sometimes cover the cost of accessing and repairing a sudden slab leak and the resulting damage, though policies differ widely. Ask before the floor is opened.

Codes, permits and what to ask

Repair pipe used under or beside a slab is made to ASTM standards, and plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC list approved materials and how they are supported and protected.

Ask the building department at 205-655-5483 whether your repair needs a permit, and ask your plumber to name the pipe material going in.
