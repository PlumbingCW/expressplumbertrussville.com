Sewer Inspection in Trussville, AL

A camera shows what is wrong inside a sewer line without digging. Call before a purchase, a repair or a repeat backup.

Call (205) 896-0120

What a sewer camera shows

We feed a waterproof camera on a flexible cable into the line through a cleanout. As it moves along, it records the pipe walls, joints, roots, buildup, offsets and low spots where water sits.

The video gives you a clear picture of the condition of the pipe. It also helps us tell you whether the line needs cleaning, a spot repair or a full replacement, so you are not guessing.

What a visit looks like

Most inspections are done in an hour or two, depending on the line.

  1. We locate your cleanout, or find another access point if there is none.
  2. The camera runs through the line to the public connection.
  3. We watch live, record the run and mark any trouble spots.
  4. You get a plain-language summary of what we saw and what it means.

What the camera can find

These are the most common findings.

FindingWhat it meansLikely next step
Roots in jointsGrowth entering through gapsCutting, jetting, and joint repair if needed.
Belly or sagLow point that holds waterMonitor or repair the section.
Crack or breakStructural damageSpot repair or replacement.
Heavy scale or greaseBuildup on pipe wallsCleaning or jetting.
Offset jointPipes out of lineRepair at the joint.

Reading the video with you

A recording is more useful when somebody explains it. We pause on the problem spots and show you where they sit in relation to the house, the yard and the public connection.

That context makes the next decision easier. A short crack near the cleanout is a very different job from a long belly under the driveway, even if both look similar on screen.

When the camera is not the first step

If a line is fully blocked, the camera cannot pass, and cleaning comes first. If a line is backing up right now, stop using water and read our page on sewer line cleaning.

Once the line is open again, the camera can show what caused the backup and whether it is likely to return.

Before buying a home

A standard home inspection usually does not include a camera run through the sewer line. If the house is older, has mature trees near the line or has a history of backups, the camera can save you from a costly surprise after closing.

Trussville has homes from many eras, including neighborhoods built in the 1930s and 40s along Chalkville Road and newer subdivisions from the I-59 and I-459 growth years. Pipe materials differ across those ages, and the camera shows what each house actually has.

What affects the price of a sewer inspection

Ask for a clear price before the visit. These factors set it.

Access

A cleanout makes the visit simple, but without one we may need to pull a toilet.

Line length

Longer runs take more time.

Cleaning first

A line packed with debris may need cleaning before the camera can pass.

Report and video

We can provide a recording and written notes.

Timing

Scheduled visits cost less than rush calls.

Some insurers ask for a camera report before covering a repair. Ask your agent.

Questions about the public side

Your property line separates your pipe from the public sewer. Trussville Gas and Water lists sewer information on its site and can be reached at 205-655-3211. Owners in an older neighborhood such as the Cahaba Project can read our notes on what to check before ordering a camera run.

If the camera finds trouble past your property line, the utility needs to know, and we help you describe what we saw.
