Plumber in the Cahaba Project, Trussville, AL

The Cahaba Project is Trussville's historic planned village, built in the 1930s. If you own a house here, call and tell us about the drain, water heater or leak you are dealing with.

A planned village from the 1930s

The Cahaba Project was built between 1936 and 1938 by the federal Resettlement Administration on the site of the old Trussville furnace. It was planned as a village of about 287 houses and duplexes on lots of roughly half an acre to three quarters of an acre, arranged around a central green called The Mall.

The Birmingham Wiki describes the village as roughly bounded by Rockridge Avenue, the Cahaba River, Gadsden Highway and Poplar Street, with North Chalkville Road as its main road. The neighborhood is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and it became part of Trussville when the town incorporated in 1947.

For plumbing, that history matters in one way. Houses from the late 1930s have had many decades of repairs, remodels and additions, so what is inside your walls today may not match what was built. We would rather look than guess.

What the records say about the original plumbing

Here is what is documented and what is not.

  • Sources describe each original house as built with indoor plumbing, hot and cold running water, electricity and a sewer connection.
  • The federal government built the village's water system, and it was turned over to Trussville in the late 1940s.
  • No source we found says which pipe materials were used or what has been replaced since, so we do not assume anything about your lines.

Find out what your house has before you spend money

These steps cost nothing and make any plumber's visit more useful.

  1. Find your main water shutoff and make sure the handle still turns.
  2. Look at any pipe you can see in a basement, crawl space or utility area, and note the color and material. Photos help when you call.
  3. Check the water meter with every tap closed. If the dial keeps moving, water is going somewhere.
  4. Notice which drains run slowly. One slow drain and several slow drains point to different places.
  5. Call us with what you found, and we will tell you which service fits.

Before a renovation or an addition

City of Trussville Building Safety and Inspections can tell you whether a project needs a permit. Its number is 205-655-5483.

Because the neighborhood is on the National Register of Historic Places, it is also worth asking the city whether exterior changes have any review requirements. We do not know the rules for individual properties, so ask before you plan.

Who to call about the water supply

Trussville Gas and Water runs the public water supply, and its number is 205-655-3211. Ask them where the utility's responsibility ends and yours begins, then call us for anything on your side of the line.
