A cold snap, a summer storm and the pipes in between

Alabama weather swings between hard freezes and heavy summer storms, and both stress plumbing. A hard freeze can split exposed pipes and outdoor spigots, while storms can wash debris into drains and overload older sewer lines.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Leeds. Insulate exposed pipes before a freeze, keep a drain clear after a storm, and know where your main shutoff is.

Before we arrive in Leeds: if a pipe has burst

These steps apply in any season, and they matter most after a freeze.

  1. Close the main water valve at the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Open a lower faucet to drain the pipes once the main is off.
  3. Switch off power to wet rooms if water is near outlets or the panel.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell us where the pipe split, if you can see it.

Getting ready before a hard freeze

Disconnect garden hoses and, if the spigot is not frost-proof, cover it. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes, and let a faucet drip slowly on the coldest night.

Know where your main shutoff is and how to turn it. If a pipe freezes but has not yet burst, keep the faucet open and call us before you try to thaw it with a flame, which is a fire risk.

A city that spans three counties

Leeds lies in Jefferson, St. Clair and Shelby counties, and it was incorporated on April 27, 1887, built around workers at the Lehigh Portland cement plant. The Little Cahaba River runs nearby, and the city sits on I-20 at exits 140 and 144, with US-411 running to Moody.

That county mix matters because the utility and the permit office for your address depend on where it sits. Ask us if you are unsure who to call, and use the utility on your bill for anything on the public side. For shops along the corridor, see commercial plumbing.
