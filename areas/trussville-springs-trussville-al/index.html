Before you call any plumber in a newer home

A problem in a young house may be covered, so a few minutes of checking can save money.

  • 1Find your closing paperwork and the date you took possession.
  • 2Read what the builder's warranty says about plumbing and for how long.
  • 3Photograph the problem before anyone touches it.
  • 4If water is actively leaking, close the main shutoff first.
  • 5Call your builder if the warranty applies, and call us if it does not or the problem cannot wait.

A newer community by the river

Trussville Springs covers about 167 acres beside the Cahaba River, off Highway 11 northeast of downtown and near Trussville Civitan Park. It was designed as an extension of downtown Trussville, with a master plan drawn after a design workshop in 2005, and the planning board approved its first phase in June 2006.

The Birmingham Wiki reports that by 2016 the community had about 46 cottage homes, 10 river homes and 15 townhome units, plus a pool, fishing pond, trail and storm shelter. New home listing sites show builders still selling here.

Because most homes are young, plumbing questions here tend to concern workmanship, warranty and appliance setup more than old pipe.

Townhomes and shared walls

Trussville Springs includes townhome units as well as cottages and river homes. In a townhome, a leak can involve a shared wall or a neighbor's unit, so tell us which unit and which wall is wet.

If you have a homeowners association or property manager, ask what it maintains before you assume a shared line is yours.

A wet yard by the river is not always a leak

Some homes here back onto the Cahaba River, and rain can leave a lot wet for reasons that have nothing to do with plumbing. To tell the difference, close every tap and watch the water meter.

A still meter points to surface water, and a moving meter means water is escaping somewhere, which is when to call us.
