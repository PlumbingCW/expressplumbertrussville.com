Backed-up drains and slow lines in Pinson homes

On older streets, slow drains and repeat clogs are common warning signs of an aging line. A drain that slows in more than one fixture points to the main line, and a single slow sink usually means a local clog.

  • Gurgling when you flush or run the washer.
  • Water rising in a tub after a toilet flush.
  • A sewer smell near a floor drain.
  • Standing water after every rain in the yard drain.

Why Pinson's history matters for your pipes

Pinson was incorporated in 2004, and its Palmerdale area was one of the New Deal resettlement communities, with the first of 102 homesteads finished in 1935. Houses from that era may still be standing, and with them can come original or old pipe.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Pinson, and for an older house we like a camera look first. Read about sewer inspection if you are buying an older place, or about repiping if the supply lines show rust.

Before we arrive in Pinson: if a pipe has burst or a drain is overflowing

Follow these steps while we are on the way.

  1. Shut the main water valve if water is spraying, or stop using water if a drain is overflowing.
  2. Switch off power to the wet area at the panel if water has reached outlets.

Cast iron, clay and other old drain pipes

General industry knowledge says drain lines in houses from the 1930s to the 1960s were often cast iron or clay tile. Cast iron rusts and scales from the inside, and clay tile joints let roots in.

Neither failure shows from the surface. You notice repeat clogs, a sewer smell or a patch of wet lawn, and a camera tells us which one you have. If the pipe is sound, cleaning may be enough, and if it is cracked, a repair is the honest answer.

Buying or selling an older Pinson home

A standard home inspection usually does not run a camera through the sewer line. For a house that predates the 1980s, that camera look is a sensible extra before closing.

Sellers can use it too, since a clean report answers a buyer's question before it becomes a negotiation. We record the run and explain what we see in plain language.

A creek-side lot and your yard drain

Turkey Creek Nature Preserve is a well-known Pinson landmark. If your lot sits on low ground near a creek, a wet yard after rain may be drainage, not a plumbing leak.

A quick way to tell them apart is to check the water meter with every tap closed. If it stays still, the wetness is surface water, and if it moves, call us.
