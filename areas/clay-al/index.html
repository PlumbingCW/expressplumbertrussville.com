Before we arrive in Clay: if a pipe has burst or a leak is active

Do these in order. They take a couple of minutes and limit the damage.

  1. Close the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off power to wet rooms at the breaker panel if water is near outlets.
  3. Clear furniture and rugs from the wet area, and lift what you can off the floor.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell us the house age and where the water is coming from.

A two-minute meter test for a hidden leak

Turn off every tap and appliance that uses water, then look at your water meter. If the small dial or the low-flow indicator keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

Check a toilet next by adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank. If color shows up in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is the leak, which is an easy repair. If neither test explains the movement, the leak is hidden, and that is when we use leak detection tools instead of opening walls.

Insurance and what to document after water damage

Take photos and video of the damage before you clean up, and keep the damaged items until your insurer says otherwise. Write down the time you found the leak and when you shut the water off.

Sudden damage from a burst pipe is often covered, while slow leaks are often treated as maintenance, though policies differ. Ask your agent before you tear out flooring, and keep any receipts for emergency repairs.
