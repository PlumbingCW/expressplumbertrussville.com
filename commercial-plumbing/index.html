When a commercial plumbing problem cannot wait

A backed-up floor drain, a failed water heater in a kitchen, a burst line in the ceiling or a restroom with no water can close a business for the day. Our line is open 24/7 and we book the earliest visit we can.

Before we arrive:

  1. Shut off the water at the main valve if water is spraying or flooding.
  2. Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and wastewater.
  3. Move stock and equipment out of the wet area if it is safe to do so.
  4. Call your landlord or property manager if the lease assigns the plumbing to them.

Grease, traps and kitchen drains

A restaurant kitchen sends grease down the line all day. A grease trap catches much of it, but only if it is cleaned on schedule, and the line beyond it still needs hydro jetting now and then.

We can look at your trap and line, and recommend a cleaning schedule based on what we find. Local rules on traps and discharge vary, so ask the building department or utility about your obligations.

Restrooms, water heaters and daily operations

Public restrooms see heavy use, so flush valves, toilets and faucets wear faster than in a home. A schedule of checks catches small leaks before they raise your water bill or close a stall.

Commercial kitchens, salons and gyms rely on hot water all day. If the heater fails mid-service, we can repair it or recommend a replacement sized to the load, and for planned upgrades see water heater replacement.

Planning ahead with a maintenance visit

Many commercial emergencies start as small issues that nobody had time to look at. A short visit to check shutoff valves, floor drains, traps and backflow devices tells you where the weak points are.

Know where your main shutoff is, label it, and make sure every manager on shift can find it. Those two steps can turn a ruined weekend into a minor cleanup.

Backflow prevention and storm drains

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination, and many commercial buildings need them on irrigation, fire and process lines. They normally need testing at set intervals, and the water utility sets those rules.

Storm drains in parking lots and loading areas clog with leaves and debris. Trussville has businesses along corridors like Gadsden Highway, Chalkville Road and Trussville Crossings Parkway, and a clogged lot drain can flood a doorway in a hard rain.

Backflow rules and your utility

Plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC require backflow protection on connections like irrigation and fire lines, and the water utility sets how often assemblies are tested.

Ask the utility at 205-655-3211 which devices in your building need testing, and keep the records.
