Repiping in Trussville, AL

Pinhole leaks, rusty water and falling pressure often mean the pipes are wearing out. Call to see whether repiping makes sense.

Call (205) 896-0120

Signs your pipes may be at the end of their life

Rust-colored water, falling pressure, repeated pinhole leaks and stained walls or ceilings all suggest worn supply lines. One leak does not mean the whole house needs repiping, but a pattern of leaks does.

Older homes in and around Trussville may hold galvanized steel or older copper. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside, which narrows the opening and lets pressure fall. Knowing which material is in your walls is the first step.

Common supply pipe materials

Each material has its own track record.

MaterialWhere it shows upWhat to know
Galvanized steelOlder homesCorrodes inside, restricts flow.
CopperMany mid-century and later homesDurable, but can pinhole in some water.
PEXNewer homes and repipesFlexible, quick to install, resists freezing damage better.
PolybutyleneSome homes from the late 1970s to mid-1990sGeneral industry knowledge says it has a poor failure record, so have it assessed.
CPVCSome newer homesRigid plastic, can become brittle over time.

Repipe or repair as you go

If one section is failing and the rest is sound, repairing the section may be enough. If leaks keep appearing in different spots, each repair opens more wall and the costs add up.

A repipe replaces the uncertainty with new lines and a fresh start. We help you weigh the cost of repeat repairs against the cost of one planned project.

Water quality and your pipes

Trussville Gas and Water's 2026 report lists lead at 1 ppb and copper at 0.28 ppm at the 90th percentile from 2023 sampling. Those figures describe a sample across the system and not what is in your pipes.

If your home has older plumbing and you are concerned, ask us what the pipes are made of and consider a water test from a certified lab.

What a repipe involves

We plan the new routes, protect your finishes, open only the walls we must and install new lines to every fixture. Water is off for part of the job, and we plan the order of work to keep the outage as short as we can.

Drywall is patched at the end, though finish painting is usually left to you or a painter. We talk through every step before work begins, including how long you should expect to be without water.

The repipe process in order

The sequence is the same in most houses.

  1. We inspect the existing pipes and agree on a plan.
  2. New supply lines are run to every fixture and connected to the main.
  3. The system is pressure tested before any wall is closed.
  4. Walls are patched and the old lines are removed or capped.

Living through a repipe

Expect some noise, dust and moved furniture, and expect water to be off for part of each working day. We lay protection over floors, and we agree with you on which walls will be opened before we start.

If you have a pet or a family member with medical needs, tell us ahead of time. We can plan the order of rooms around that.

What affects repiping cost

House size

More bathrooms and fixtures mean more pipe.

Pipe material

PEX and copper are priced differently.

Access

Slab homes, finished basements and two stories change the work.

Wall repair

Drywall patching and finishing add to the scope.

Permit

Ask the building department at 205-655-5483.

Insurance may help with damage from a sudden burst but usually not with the cost of replacing worn pipes. Ask your agent.

Drain pipes are a separate question

Repiping covers the supply side, the lines that bring water in. Old cast iron drain pipes behind the walls are a different system, and they can corrode or crack with age.

If you see slow drains and rust at the joints, read about sewer line repair or ask us to include the drain lines in the assessment.

Approved pipe materials

PEX and copper supply pipe are made to ASTM standards, and plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC list which materials are approved for drinking water systems.

Ask which material your plumber recommends and why, and whether the permit and inspection are included.
