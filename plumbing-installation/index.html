Plumbing Installation in Trussville, AL

New fixtures, added bathrooms and relocated lines all start with a clear plan. Call to talk through your project.

What we install

Installation covers the fixtures you see and the lines you do not. That includes sinks, toilets, tubs and showers, outdoor spigots, washing machine boxes, ice maker lines, shutoff valves and new supply or drain runs for remodels and additions.

If you are planning a remodel, bring us in early. Moving a drain or adding a bathroom is far easier before the walls are closed, and the layout often decides what is possible.

How an installation project runs

The order helps the job go smoothly.

  1. We talk through your plan, check the existing lines and confirm what is possible.
  2. We rough in supply, drain and vent lines where new work is needed.
  3. Fixtures are set, connected and tested for leaks.
  4. We walk you through shutoffs and clean up the work area.

Planning before the walls open

The cheapest time to move a line is before the drywall is up. Think about where you want fixtures, what size tub or shower you want and whether a future bathroom is on your list.

We can rough in lines now for a future addition, which costs less than opening finished walls later. We also look at your main shutoff and pressure to make sure the new work does not stress old pipes.

Choosing fixtures that last

A good fixture has solid internal parts and a finish that stands up to your water. Ask about replacement cartridges and whether the manufacturer still stocks them.

Trussville Gas and Water's 2026 report lists total hardness as 205 without a printed unit. Hard water can wear finishes and clog aerators, so choose fixtures that are easy to clean and service.

What to do if a new fitting leaks after installation

A leak at a new connection is usually a loose fitting or a missed seal, and it is worth fixing quickly.

  • Close the shutoff valve to that fixture, or the main valve if there is none.
  • Dry the area and look for the exact connection that is wet.
  • Do not overtighten a compression fitting, which can crack it.
  • Call us and describe what you see, and we will tell you whether it can wait.

Venting and drains matter as much as supply

A drain needs a vent to move wastewater without gurgling or trapping odor, and the right slope to carry waste away. Mistakes here show up later as slow drains and smells.

That is why we plan the whole run, not just the fixture. If an addition ties into an older line, we check that the existing drain can handle the added load. If it might not, we say so.

Shutoffs, pressure and the small parts

Every fixture should have its own shutoff valve, so a future repair does not mean turning off the whole house. Many older homes lack them, and adding them is one of the most useful small upgrades.

We also check water pressure at the same time. High pressure wears fixtures and supply lines, and a pressure regulator near the main is often the fix.

Permits and licensed work

City of Trussville Building Safety and Inspections can tell you whether a project needs a permit. The Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board has a licensee search for anyone you plan to hire. If your home is in a newer community such as Trussville Springs, check your builder's warranty before you plan any changes.

Doing both checks before work begins protects you, particularly for a remodel that affects resale.

Why we ask about the code first

New plumbing work is governed by the plumbing code your jurisdiction adopts, usually based on the IPC or the UPC. Venting, trap distances and fixture clearances all come from it.

Checking it before work begins is cheaper than correcting a layout after the walls close.
