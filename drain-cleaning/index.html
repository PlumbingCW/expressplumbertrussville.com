When a clogged drain is an emergency

Water coming up out of a floor drain is a warning. So is a toilet that gurgles when the washer runs, or a kitchen sink full of standing water. Each usually means the clog has moved past the fixture and into a branch or main line. Our line is open 24/7, nights and weekends included, and we book the earliest visit we can.

Until a plumber arrives, these steps keep a bad backup from getting worse:

  1. Stop running water. Pause the dishwasher and washer, because every gallon you add has nowhere to go.
  2. Skip the chemical drain cleaner. Poured into standing water it can splash back, and it leaves caustic liquid sitting in the pipe for whoever opens it next.
  3. Pull rugs and anything valuable away from the floor drain or tub, and keep kids and pets out of the wet area.
  4. If water is near outlets or the panel, leave the room. Cut power only if the breaker box is in a dry spot you can reach without stepping in water.

Why drains clog in Trussville homes

Trussville grew in layers. The tree-lined streets off Chalkville Road date to the 1930s and 40s, and the Cahaba Project village went up in 1938 with indoor plumbing as part of its 287 units. The biggest surge came when I-59 and I-459 were finished, and the 1985 to 1987 annexation tripled the city's land area.

That history matters under the floor. Older cast iron or clay drain lines roughen with age and catch grease and tree roots, while newer PVC lines tend to clog from what went down them. A plumber who knows which era your house comes from knows where to start looking, which saves you from paying to clear the wrong line.

Where the water backs up tells us where the clog sits. This table is how we read the symptoms before we pick a tool.

A camera look is worth the cost when a clog repeats, because snaking a broken pipe only clears the symptom for a few weeks.

Snaking, jetting or a bigger fix

A cable machine bores through a soft clog like hair, paper or a wad of food, and it is the right first tool for most sinks and tubs. When the pipe wall itself is coated with grease or tree roots, a cable leaves a thin channel and the line clogs again, so a scoured pipe does more good than a punched hole.

That is where hydro jetting earns its place, and where sewer line cleaning of the whole main makes sense. If the camera shows a collapsed or offset section, no amount of cleaning will hold, and we will tell you plainly that a sewer line repair is the real fix.

What affects the price of drain cleaning

Ask for a clear price before work starts. These are the things that move that number, so you know what is behind it.

Where the clog is

A fixture trap is a quick job, while a main line clog needs bigger equipment and more time.

The tool needed

A hand snake costs less than a drum cable machine, and a jetter costs more than both.

Access

A cleanout in the yard is easy to reach. A line with no cleanout may mean pulling a toilet to get in.

Time of day

Nights, weekends and holidays are emergency calls, and the price reflects that before you agree to anything.

Whether it repeats

A clog that keeps returning may need a camera inspection added to the visit.

Insurance usually does not pay for clearing a clog. A backup that damages flooring may be covered if your policy includes a water backup endorsement, so read your policy or ask your agent before the cleanup starts.

Public main or your own line

Trussville Gas and Water lists sewer information on its website, and the first question after a backup is whose pipe it is. If a neighbor has the same problem or the water backs up from the street side, call the utility as well, because the blockage may be in the public main and not in your line.

A plumber can usually tell you in a short visit whether the clog sits on your side. If it does, we clear it. If it does not, you will know who to call, and you will not have paid for work on the wrong pipe.

The code behind drain traps and vents

Every fixture drain is required to have a trap, a water seal that blocks sewer gas, and plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC set how that trap is vented. That is why a slow drain with gurgling can be a venting question and not only a clog.

The code your jurisdiction has adopted decides the details, so ask the building department if you are changing a drain layout.
