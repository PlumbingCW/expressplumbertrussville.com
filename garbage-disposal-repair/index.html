Emergency Garbage Disposal Repair in Trussville, AL

A disposal that hums but will not spin, or leaks into the cabinet, can often be fixed in one visit. Call the 24/7 line if water is pooling.

When a disposal problem is urgent

A leaking disposal wets the cabinet floor and a backed-up sink makes the kitchen unusable. Our line is open 24/7 and we book the earliest visit we can.

Before we arrive:

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall. Never put a hand into the chamber, even when it is off.
  2. If it leaks, empty the cabinet and place a bowl under the unit.
  3. If the sink is full, stop running water and avoid flushing the dishwasher.
  4. Do not pour drain chemicals into a jammed unit, because they sit in the chamber and splash during repair.

Repair or replace

A jam, a tripped reset or a loose connection is a quick repair. A leak from the body or a motor that has burned out usually means replacement, because the housing cannot be patched reliably.

If your disposal is more than ten years old and has begun to leak, replacing it is often the better choice. We can install a new one the same visit, and we talk through horsepower and features with you before you buy.

Choosing a replacement unit

Disposals come in a range of motor sizes, and a bigger motor handles heavier use with less jamming. A family that cooks daily is better served by a mid-range or larger unit than a basic one.

Batch-feed models run only when the stopper is locked in place, which some households prefer for safety. We help you pick something that fits your sink, your cabinet space and how you use the kitchen.

A simple routine that extends the life of a unit

Run cold water before, during and for several seconds after each use, so scraps clear the trap and the line. Grind small amounts at a time instead of loading the chamber.

Citrus peels and ice are fine in moderation and help the chamber stay fresh. A disposal that smells usually needs a cleaning, not a replacement.

What a disposal should and should not take

Small food scraps and plenty of cold running water are what a disposal is built for. Grease, coffee grounds in quantity, fibrous items like celery, and starchy foods such as rice and pasta tend to pack into the drain beyond the unit.

That is how a disposal that works fine can still cause a kitchen line to clog. If the sink backs up repeatedly, see drain cleaning or hydro jetting for the stubborn cases.

Traps, codes and disposal hookups

Plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC require a trap on a sink drain, and a disposal ties into that trap. A bad connection after the disposal is one reason a repaired unit can still leak.

If your kitchen layout changes during a replacement, ask the building department whether the work needs a permit.
