What to do when a faucet leak is an emergency

A drip is an annoyance. Water spraying or soaking a cabinet is urgent. Our line is open 24/7 and we book the earliest visit we can.

Do this first:

  1. Turn the small shutoff valves under the sink clockwise to stop the supply.
  2. If a valve fails to close, shut the main water valve for the house.
  3. Empty the cabinet and dry the base before the wood swells.
  4. Photograph the damage in case you need it for an insurance claim.

Repair or replace the faucet

A repair is the right call when the body is sound and only a cartridge, washer or seal has worn. A replacement is better when the finish has failed, parts are no longer made or the faucet leaks again right after a repair.

For kitchens, we can also talk about a pull-down model if you want one. If the shutoff valves under the sink are corroded and will not close, we often replace them in the same visit so the next repair is simple.

Water spraying under the sink? Call the 24/7 line and shut the valves first.

Kitchen, bath and outdoor fixtures

Kitchen faucets work hardest, with daily use, heavy pull-down hoses and hot water that speeds wear. Bath faucets tend to fail at the cartridge, and tub spouts at the diverter, which is the small lever that sends water to the shower.

Outdoor spigots are a separate case. A frost-proof spigot has a long stem that reaches into the warm side of the wall, and a hose left attached in winter can trap water behind the valve. If a spigot drips inside the wall, shut the interior valve if you have one and call us.

Hard water and aerators

Trussville Gas and Water's 2026 report lists total hardness as 205, and the table does not print a unit. The report also says the water comes from eight wells in limestone and chert aquifers, which is the kind of source that tends to leave mineral deposits.

Those deposits clog aerators and wear cartridges over time. If a faucet flow drops in one fixture only, unscrew the aerator and rinse it, and call us if cleaning does not restore the flow. To test your own tap, ask a lab or the utility how.

Flow limits and your aerator

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, a labeled bathroom faucet is limited to 1.5 gallons per minute. The aerator at the spout is the part that sets that flow.

A faucet with a weak stream is often a clogged aerator, and rinsing it takes a minute.
