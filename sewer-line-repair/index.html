Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage in a tub or floor drain, several fixtures backing up at once, or gurgling when you flush all point to a blocked or broken main line. Our line is open 24/7 and we book the earliest visit we can. Wastewater is a health hazard, so treat it seriously.

Do these things right away:

  1. Stop using water. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher, no showers.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone vulnerable out of the affected area, and do not walk through the water in bare feet.
  3. Do not mix or pour drain chemicals into a backed-up line.
  4. Check whether neighbors have the same problem. If they do, call the utility too, because the blockage may be in the public main.

What fails in a sewer line

Older homes in Trussville, including those near the tree-lined streets off Chalkville Road that date to the 1930s and 40s, may have cast iron or clay lines. Those materials crack, flake and separate at joints with age, and roots find the openings. Newer PVC lines fail differently, usually from a poor slope, a crushed section or a joint that was never glued properly.

Whatever the material, the fix starts with seeing the pipe. A sewer camera inspection tells us where it is broken, how long the damage runs and which repair makes sense, so you are not paying to dig where nothing is wrong.

Repair options and when each makes sense

Digging is not the only route. These are the usual choices.

MethodWhat it isFits when
Spot repairDig and replace one damaged sectionOne short break or offset, with clear access.
Pipe liningA resin liner cured inside the old pipeCracks or root intrusion in a pipe still roughly straight and intact.
Full replacementNew pipe along the old path or a new routeCollapsed, bellied or widely damaged pipe.
Cleaning onlySewer line cleaning or jettingNo structural damage, just buildup.

Lining and spot repairs usually disturb less lawn than a full dig, but they are not right for every pipe. We say plainly when they are not.

Before you dig, call 811

Before any digging, call 811, the national call-before-you-dig number, so buried utility lines get marked. A locate request protects gas, electric, water and communications lines, and it is a step we plan for on any dig. Never start digging in the yard yourself without it.

If your problem started with a stubborn clog, drain cleaning or hydro jetting may clear it for now, but a repeat backup is the signal to look at the pipe itself.
