Who Installs Garbage Disposals in Trussville, AL?

By the Trussville Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-13.

A plumber is the usual choice, since a disposal ties into the sink drain, and sometimes the wiring too. Here is who does what and what to confirm before the work starts.

Call (205) 896-0120

The short answer

A licensed plumber is the most common installer, because the job is mostly plumbing. The disposal bolts to the sink flange, connects to the trap and drain, and often links to the dishwasher drain.

The electrical part is small but real. A disposal either plugs into an outlet under the sink or is wired to a switch, and if there is no outlet or switch yet, an electrician may be needed. Many plumbers handle simple plug-in setups, and some bring in an electrician for new wiring.

What the installation involves

Plans vary by sink, but the work follows a pattern.

  1. Switch off the power at the breaker, then remove the old unit or the sink strainer.
  2. Mount the new unit to the sink flange with the supplied mounting ring and snap ring.
  3. Connect the discharge to the trap, add the dishwasher drain line and knock out the dishwasher plug if the dishwasher drains through the disposal.
  4. Wire or plug in the unit, restore power, run water and test for leaks and noise.

The dishwasher knockout plug

Many new disposals have a small plastic plug inside the dishwasher inlet. If the dishwasher drains through the disposal, that plug has to be knocked out. If it is forgotten, the dishwasher will not drain properly and may back up.

This is the most common installation mistake, and it is easy to miss. A good installer checks it every time, and you can ask them to confirm.

Choosing the right disposal

Disposals range from basic units with a small motor to larger ones with more power and sound insulation. A household that cooks daily usually does better with a mid-range or larger unit than with the cheapest one.

Continuous-feed models run while the switch is on, and batch-feed models run only when the stopper is in place. Pick what suits your household, and measure the cabinet space, since some larger models are tall.

Replacing an old unit versus adding a first one

A like-for-like replacement is the simplest job, because the plumbing and wiring are already in place. Adding a first disposal to a sink that never had one means changing the drain, possibly adding a switch and checking the sink opening.

If you have a double sink, the disposal normally goes on the side with the drain that connects to the trap arm. Ask the installer how the existing drain will connect, and whether the trap needs to be changed.

What to check before you hire

Ask whether the person is a licensed plumber, and check them with the Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board's licensee search. Ask what the price includes, such as haul-away of the old unit, new connections and testing.

Ask about warranty. Disposal manufacturers often offer a multi-year warranty on the unit, and the installer should explain what their labor warranty covers.

Doing it yourself, and when not to

Swapping one disposal for another is a reasonable weekend project for a handy homeowner, since the mounting hardware is usually included. The tricky parts are the electrical connection, a stuck old mounting ring and a drain trap that does not line up.

If the old unit is corroded in place, the sink flange has to come out and be resealed with plumber's putty or a sealant. A poor seal at the flange is the leak we see most often after a DIY job.

What a good installation looks like

The unit hangs level and does not wobble, the flange seal is tight and dry, and the drain connections do not leak when the sink is full. The dishwasher line connects high on the disposal, and the plug has been knocked out if needed.

Run the disposal with water for a minute and look underneath with a flashlight. Check again after an hour and the next morning, because slow drips show up late.

What to keep out of the new unit

Grease, coffee grounds in quantity, bones, fruit pits, fibrous scraps like celery and starchy foods such as rice and pasta can all cause trouble in the unit or in the line beyond it.

Always run cold water before, during and after use. A disposal handles small scraps well, and overloading it is the quickest way to turn a new unit into a repair call.

Questions to ask on the phone

Ask whether the quote includes the unit or only labor, whether the old disposal is hauled away and whether the dishwasher connection is included. Ask who handles any electrical work, and whether a leak test is part of the visit.

A clear answer to each of these tells you most of what you need to know about the installer. Vague answers, or a price that changes when the person arrives, are a signal to keep looking.

Permits and the public sewer

For permit questions on any plumbing work, the City of Trussville's Department of Building Safety and Inspections is at 205-655-5483. A like-for-like replacement is usually minor, but the office can confirm for your address.

Trussville Gas and Water lists sewer information on its website and can be reached at 205-655-3211. Disposals add solids to your drain line, which is another reason to keep grease and fibrous scraps out.

Codes that apply to a disposal

Plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC require a trap and proper venting on a sink drain, and a disposal ties into that trap. The unit's manual lists the electrical and drain requirements.

A careful installer works from both, and checks that the dishwasher connection is correct.
