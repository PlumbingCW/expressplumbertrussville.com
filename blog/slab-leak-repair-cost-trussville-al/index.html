Slab Leak Repair Cost in Trussville, AL

By the Trussville Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-13.

A slab leak is a pipe leak under a concrete floor, and the bill depends mostly on how the plumber reaches it. Here is what published cost guides say and what changes the number.

Call (205) 896-0120

The short answer on slab leak repair cost

Published national guides put slab leak repair at roughly $630 to $4,400 or more, with an average near $2,300, according to This Old House's cost guide updated in April 2026. Those are national figures, not Trussville quotes, and they cover repair only.

Finding the leak is a separate step. The same guide lists slab leak detection at about $150 to $600. Anyone who quotes you a flat number without seeing the house is guessing, so treat a phone price as a starting point and ask for the scope in writing.

Three ways to fix a slab leak, and when each fits

The method decides most of the cost, which is why two homes with the same leak can get very different bills.

MethodWhat it involvesRelative costBest fit
Spot repairCut the slab over the leak, fix the pipe, patch the concreteModerateOne clear leak in an otherwise sound line.
RerouteAbandon the leaking line and run new pipe through walls or the atticModerate to higherLeak is hard to reach or the old line is failing.
RepipeReplace the whole supply systemHigherRepeat leaks or old pipe throughout, see repiping.

What moves the price up or down

Every house is different, but these five details explain most of the spread between quotes.

Where the leak is

A leak under a bare garage floor is cheaper to reach than one under tile, a kitchen island or a bathtub.

Floor finish

Tile, hardwood and carpet all need to be removed and put back, and matching old tile can be hard.

Pipe material

Copper, PEX and older galvanized lines each repair differently.

How many leaks

A single failure is a spot repair. Several along one line usually point to a reroute.

Timing

Night and weekend calls are quoted as emergency work.

Why detection comes before any digging

Concrete hides the pipe, so the leak is rarely where the wet spot is. Water follows the easiest path under the slab, and a damp patch in one room can come from a pipe several feet away.

Acoustic listening gear, thermal imaging and pressure tests narrow the search to a small area before anyone cuts. Our page on slab leak repair explains each method, and leak detection covers the tools. Paying for detection first almost always costs less than opening the wrong spot.

What a typical slab leak bill is made of

A slab leak invoice usually has four parts: finding the leak, getting to it, fixing the pipe and putting the floor back. Detection is the smallest piece, the plumbing repair is the middle, and restoration of tile, wood or carpet can be the part that surprises people.

Ask who handles each piece. Many plumbers repair the pipe and patch the concrete, then leave finish flooring to a flooring contractor, so budget separately for it. If the quote lumps everything together, ask for a line-by-line breakdown.

Why cheaper is not always cheaper

A very low quote may skip detection, patch only the concrete or leave an aging line in place. If the pipe is corroded in several places, a spot repair can be followed by a second leak a few feet away.

Ask the plumber to look at the pipe's condition when it is exposed and to tell you honestly whether the rest of the line looks sound. That conversation is worth more than a small difference in price.

How to avoid paying for the same dig twice

A few habits keep a repair from turning into a second one.

  1. Confirm the leak is under the slab. A meter test with every tap closed tells you whether water is moving.
  2. Ask the plumber to locate the leak with equipment before quoting the repair.
  3. Get the method, the patch and the floor restoration described in writing, including who does the flooring.
  4. Ask whether the old line is likely to fail again, because rerouting can cost less than repairing the same pipe twice.

Insurance and slab leaks

Policies differ, and many treat the repair of the pipe itself as maintenance while covering sudden water damage that follows. Some policies cover the cost of getting to the pipe, often called tear-out, and some do not.

Call your agent before work starts, take photos of any damage and keep every invoice. If a leak has run for months, an insurer may treat it differently from a sudden burst, so ask about the wording on long-term leaks.

Signs your leak may be under the slab

Warm spots on the floor point to a hot water line, and the sound of running water with every tap off suggests a pressurized leak. Damp carpet at a wall base, cracked or lifting tile, a musty smell and a steady rise in the water bill are other common signs.

None of these proves a slab leak on its own. A leaking toilet, an irrigation line or a failing water heater can look the same. That is why we start with a meter test and move to equipment only when the simple causes are ruled out.

What to do if you suspect a slab leak

Acting early limits the damage while you work out the cause.

  • Close the main water valve at the house and check the meter again.
  • Move rugs and valuables off wet or warm floor areas.
  • Photograph the damage and note when you first saw it.
  • Call a plumber before you cut or pull up any flooring.

Call 811 before any digging outside

If the repair involves digging in the yard, such as a rerouted service line, call 811 first. The national call-before-you-dig number gets buried utility lines marked, and it applies to any trench or hole.

For permit questions on larger plumbing work, the City of Trussville's Department of Building Safety and Inspections is at 205-655-5483. Ask the office whether your job needs a permit before it starts.

Where these cost figures come from

Local prices depend on access, floor finish and the method, so treat them as context and get a written scope for your own house.
