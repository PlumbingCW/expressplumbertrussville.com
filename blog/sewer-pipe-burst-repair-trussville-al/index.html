Burst Sewer Pipe Repair in Trussville, AL

By the Trussville Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-13.

A burst sewer pipe sends wastewater into the yard or under the house. Here are the signs, what to do first and how the repair options compare.

Call (205) 896-0120

Signs of a burst or collapsed sewer pipe

A broken sewer line often shows itself in the yard before it shows up in the house.

  • Soggy or sunken patches of lawn with no rain.
  • Sewer smell outdoors near the foundation.
  • Slow drains throughout the house or backups in the lowest drain.
  • A sudden burst of new green growth along the pipe route.

What to do right away

Do these in order while you arrange a repair.

  1. Stop using water in the house. Every flush and shower sends more wastewater toward the break.
  2. Keep people and pets away from any wet or sunken ground in the yard.
  3. Photograph the area and note the time you first saw trouble, for any insurance claim.
  4. Call the 24/7 line, and call 811 before any digging so buried lines get marked.

What actually breaks a sewer pipe

Tree roots finding a joint, pipe that has corroded or cracked with age, ground that has shifted and heavy loads above the line all cause breaks. Old cast iron rusts through from the inside, and clay tile cracks at joints.

In some cases a pipe has a long-term belly, a low spot where water and waste settle, and it eventually fails there. A camera look shows which problem you have, and that decides the repair.

Why a camera comes first

Before anyone digs, a camera run shows where the break is and what the rest of the line looks like. The video helps you decide between a small repair and a larger one, and it keeps you from digging in the wrong spot.

Our page on sewer inspection explains the process, and sewer line repair covers the repair options in detail. In an emergency, the camera often follows the first cleaning.

Why cleaning alone may not be enough

If the pipe has collapsed or broken, clearing the blockage gives short relief, and the line fails again. A cleaning is the right first step to restore service, and a repair is the answer to the cause.

If the line backs up repeatedly, tell the plumber about the history. That context helps decide whether sewer line cleaning is a stopgap or the whole answer.

Insurance and a burst sewer line

Policies differ. Many standard homeowner policies exclude the cost of repairing the pipe itself, and some cover damage inside the house from a sudden backup only with an endorsement.

Check your policy before you begin and take photos of everything. Some cities and utilities offer optional service line protection programs, and your agent or the utility can tell you whether one applies.

Call 811 before any digging

Alabama's one-call service, reached by dialing 811, sends locators to mark buried gas, electric, water and communications lines before you dig. It covers any trench, from a spot repair to a full replacement.

Marking takes a short time, so call as soon as you know a repair needs digging. We plan this step into every project that involves excavation, and it protects both the crew and your neighbors.

What trenchless lining can and cannot do

Lining fits a resin-soaked sleeve inside the old pipe and cures it into a new inner pipe, which avoids digging up the yard. It works for cracks and root entry in a pipe that is still in line.

It does not fix a collapsed or badly offset pipe, a belly that holds water or a line with a sharp break. The camera tells us whether lining is an option, and we say so honestly when it is not.

Restoring the yard after the repair

Any digging leaves a trench to backfill and a lawn to restore. We keep the trench as narrow as the work allows and compact the soil as it goes back, and settling is common after heavy rain.

Plan on topping up the soil and reseeding or re-sodding. Ask the plumber how restoration is handled and who does it, since landscaping, driveways and walks are often separate from the plumbing.

What a repair estimate should include

A clear estimate names the repair method, the length of pipe being replaced, the depth of the dig and what is done with the soil and old pipe. It says whether permits, 811 locates and restoration are included.

Ask what happens if the crew finds more damage than the camera showed. A good plumber explains how any change would be priced before doing the extra work.

Public line or your line

The line from your house to the property line is generally yours. Beyond that, the public sewer belongs to the utility, and Trussville Gas and Water lists sewer information on its website and answers calls at 205-655-3211.

If neighbors on your street have backups at the same time, tell the utility. If only your house is affected, the break is likely in your own lateral.

Older neighborhoods and aging lines

Trussville has neighborhoods from the Chalkville Road streets of the 1930s and 40s and subdivisions from the 1980s growth around I-59 and I-459. Sewer pipe material and condition vary widely across those eras.

That is why a camera look is worth the cost on an older house. It shows the actual pipe, and it tells you whether a repair will hold or the whole run is near the end of its life.
